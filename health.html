The research note: ACSM explicitly recognizes that research on female exercise physiology still has real gaps, so treat the phase-by-phase tips above as a loose guide, not a rulebook. The single most useful principle is autoregulation — train according to your cycle, but don't let your cycle dictate your training. Feel strong on a "rest" day? Go for it. Feel wiped on a "go hard" day? Scale back. The Foundations section below (consistent nutrition, adequate energy, strength + cardio, sleep) matters far more than which day of your cycle it is.

📚 Health Foundations — Every Day, Every Phase

A healthy woman in her 20s doesn't need a totally different plan for every week of her cycle. These basics — consistent nutrition, adequate energy, strength + cardio, sleep, and preventive care — do more for your health than any phase-specific tweak. General guidance only, not a personalized medical prescription.

🍽️ Daily Nutrient Targets

Protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg if training regularly
Fiber 25–30+ g/day
Fat 20–35% of calories, mostly unsaturated
Iron 18 mg/day (menstruating, 19–50)
Calcium 1,000 mg/day
Vitamin D 600 IU (15 mcg)/day
Magnesium 310 mg/day (19–30)
Folate 400 mcg DFE/day

Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, ACOG, U.S. Dietary Guidelines.

🏋️ Exercise Foundations

Cardio: 150 min/week moderate, or 75 min/week vigorous (CDC)

Strength: ≥2 days/week — 3–4 is great for most people

Movement: Walking daily is massively underrated

Train the 5 movement patterns:
Squat: Squat, leg press Hinge: Deadlift, hip thrust Push: Bench, push-ups Pull: Rows, pulldowns Carry/Core: Farmer carries, planks

Source: CDC Physical Activity Guidelines for Adults.

🥗 A Normal Day of Eating

🍳 Breakfast — Eggs + whole-grain toast + avocado + berries
🥗 Lunch — Chicken + rice + broccoli + avocado
🍓 Snack — Greek yogurt + berries + nuts
🍝 Dinner — Salmon + potatoes + asparagus
🍫 Dessert — Dark chocolate, fruit, or whatever you actually enjoy

A craving isn't a moral failure — eat the chocolate.

😴 Sleep & Mental Health

Sleep target: 7–9 hours/night, consistent timing

Prioritize extra sleep when training hard, menstruating, stressed, sick, or increasing training volume.

Mental health is part of physical health too:

Relationships, hobbies, time outside, stress management, and mental health care when needed — not just protein and the gym.

ACOG notes relaxation strategies and regular sleep can help ease PMS symptoms.

💊 Supplements: Food First

Food → testing/medical need → supplement.
Not: supplements → hope they fix everything.

Commonly relevant: Vitamin D, Iron, B12, Folate, Calcium, Magnesium, Omega-3 — but only based on your actual diet, labs, and circumstances.

⚠️ Don't self-prescribe high-dose iron. Too much iron can be harmful — if you suspect deficiency, ask for a CBC/ferritin test first.

🩺 Preventive Care Checklist

🚨 Not Normal "Girl Problems" — See A Doctor

ACOG identifies these as reasons to seek medical evaluation rather than chalking it up to the cycle:

🔴 Periods <21 days apart 🔴 Periods >45 days apart 🔴 Periods lasting >7 days 🔴 Suddenly irregular after being regular 🔴 No period for ~3 months 🔴 Very heavy bleeding 🔴 Severe pain that interferes with life 🔴 Fainting/dizziness with periods 🔴 Persistent fatigue 🔴 Repeated stress fractures 🔴 Major unexplained weight loss 🔴 Persistent pelvic pain

A healthy cycle is a health signal, not an inconvenience — losing it (e.g. from under-eating + over-exercising) is a sign to check in with a clinician, not a sign your routine is "working."

This page is general educational information, not a personalized medical diagnosis or treatment plan. If something feels wrong, talk to a healthcare provider.
